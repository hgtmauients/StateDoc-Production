What Is a GLP-1 and How Does It Work?

GLP-1 stands for glucagon-like peptide-1, a naturally occurring hormone in your body that plays a key role in regulating blood sugar and appetite. GLP-1 receptor agonists are a class of medications that mimic this hormone, helping your body:

  • Reduce hunger by signaling fullness to your brain
  • Slow gastric emptying, so you feel satisfied longer after meals
  • Regulate blood sugar levels, which is especially helpful for people with type 2 diabetes or insulin resistance
  • Support meaningful, sustained weight loss when combined with healthy lifestyle changes

Popular GLP-1 medications you may have heard of include semaglutide (the active ingredient in Ozempic® and Wegovy®) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro® and Zepbound®). These treatments have helped millions of people across the country — including right here in East Texas — achieve real, lasting results.

Who Is a Good Candidate for GLP-1 Therapy?

GLP-1 medications aren't one-size-fits-all, and that's exactly why a physician evaluation matters. Generally speaking, you may be a good candidate if:

You're Struggling with Excess Weight

  • Your BMI is 30 or higher (obesity)
  • Your BMI is 27 or higher with at least one weight-related health condition, such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol, or sleep apnea

You Have Metabolic Health Concerns

  • You've been diagnosed with type 2 diabetes or prediabetes
  • You have insulin resistance or metabolic syndrome
  • You've tried diet and exercise but aren't seeing the results you need

You're Ready for a Medically Supervised Approach

  • You want a structured, physician-guided plan rather than going it alone
  • You understand that GLP-1 therapy works best alongside healthy eating and regular movement

Not everyone is a candidate. Your TexasDoc physician will review your health history, current medications, and goals to determine whether a GLP-1 medication is appropriate and safe for you.

What to Expect from GLP-1 Treatment

Starting a GLP-1 medication is a process, not an overnight fix. Here's a realistic look at what the journey often looks like:

The First Few Weeks

Most GLP-1 medications are started at a low dose and gradually increased over several weeks or months. This approach helps minimize side effects and lets your body adjust. Common early side effects can include:

  • Mild nausea
  • Decreased appetite (this is actually part of how it works!)
  • Occasional fatigue
  • Digestive changes

These effects are usually temporary and tend to improve as your body adapts.

Months 1–3

Many patients begin to notice meaningful weight loss and improvements in energy levels and blood sugar control within the first few months. Results vary depending on the medication, dose, diet, and activity level.

Long-Term Success

GLP-1 therapy is most effective when it's part of a comprehensive lifestyle approach. Your TexasDoc provider can help you set realistic expectations and adjust your plan over time.
