Hair Loss Treatments That Actually Work in 2026 - Evidence-Based Guide

Pattern hair loss is common, and most of the products sold for it have never been shown to regrow hair. The treatments with a real track record are a short list: oral finasteride for men, topical minoxidil, and, for some people, a light device that has been cleared for that use. Everything else has to be judged one claim at a time.

What pattern hair loss is

Androgenetic alopecia is a change in the hair cycle on a scalp that is sensitive to dihydrotestosterone. Hairs spend less time growing and more time resting, and the new hairs come in finer. It is not the same problem as sudden shedding after illness, a thyroid disorder, iron deficiency, or a scarring alopecia. Those have different treatments, and starting a hair product without that distinction wastes months.

A visit starts by separating those patterns. Family history, the shape of the thinning, medicines that cause shedding, and recent illness or childbirth all change the next step. The treatment page for this visit is hair loss treatment.

Finasteride

Finasteride lowers dihydrotestosterone. The 1 mg oral strength is approved for men with pattern hair loss. It is taken daily, and the result is judged over many months, because the hair cycle is slow. Stopping the drug lets the previous pattern return. It is not a cosmetic that can be used on and off.

It is not prescribed to women who are pregnant or who could become pregnant, because of the risk to a male fetus. Sexual side effects, including lower libido and ejaculation changes, are on the label and have to be discussed before the first pill, not after a complaint. Mood changes are a reason to contact the prescriber. A clinician who will not mention these is skipping the drug, not simplifying it.

Minoxidil

Topical minoxidil is approved for pattern hair loss and is sold over the counter. It lengthens the growth phase. The scalp has to be the place it is applied, on the schedule printed on the product that was chosen. A different strength or a foam instead of a solution is a product choice, not a sign that more is always better.

Shedding in the first weeks is a known effect and is not, by itself, proof the drug failed. Irritation of the scalp is the usual reason people stop. Oral minoxidil is a different prescription, used for blood pressure at higher doses, and a low dose for hair is an off-label decision with its own risks, including unwanted hair growth and fluid retention. It is not the same step as buying the foam.

Light devices

Some low-level light devices are cleared for home use in pattern hair loss. Clearance of a device is not the same thing as approval of a drug, and the studies are smaller than the studies behind finasteride and minoxidil. A device can be a reasonable add-on for someone who will actually use it on a schedule. It is not a replacement for the medicines when those medicines are appropriate, and a handset sold with a percentage-of-regrowth claim is advertising.

What does not belong on the same list

Biotin corrects a biotin deficiency. It does not treat pattern hair loss in someone who is not deficient. Most shampoos, serums, and supplements in this aisle have not been tested against the natural history of the condition. Platelet-rich plasma and transplant surgery are procedures with their own evidence and their own clinics. They are not a first prescription from a telehealth visit, and a page that lists them next to finasteride as if they were interchangeable is mixing categories.

The honest timeline is months. Photos taken in the same light, at the same length, are more useful than a week-to-week impression. A clinician who started finasteride or minoxidil should be the person who decides whether to continue them.

What this article does not do

This page explains the decision. It does not approve a medication, set a dose, or replace a clinician who can see the rest of the history. Seek in-person or emergency care for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms.
