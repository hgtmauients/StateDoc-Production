What Is Ozempic and Why Are Ohioans Asking About It?

Ozempic (semaglutide) is an FDA-approved injectable medication originally developed to improve blood sugar control in adults with type 2 diabetes. It belongs to a class of drugs called GLP-1 receptor agonists, which work by mimicking a naturally occurring hormone that regulates insulin, appetite, and digestion.

Beyond diabetes management, many healthcare providers now prescribe semaglutide off-label for weight loss — particularly when combined with lifestyle changes. If you've been researching the best GLP-1 options for weight loss in Ohio, you've likely already come across Ozempic as a leading choice.

Who Qualifies for an Ozempic Prescription?

Eligibility is determined by a licensed provider during your consultation. Generally, candidates may include adults who:

  • Have a diagnosis of type 2 diabetes and need improved glycemic control
  • Have a BMI of 30 or higher (obesity), or
  • Have a BMI of 27 or higher with at least one weight-related health condition (such as hypertension, high cholesterol, or sleep apnea)
  • Have not responded adequately to diet and exercise alone
  • Do not have contraindications such as a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2)

Your provider will review your full medical history, current medications, and health goals before making any prescribing decision.

What to Expect: Dosing and Side Effects

Ozempic is administered as a once-weekly subcutaneous injection. Dosing typically begins at 0.25 mg per week for the first four weeks to minimize side effects, then increases based on your provider's guidance.

Common side effects include:
- Nausea (most common, especially early on)
- Vomiting
- Diarrhea or constipation
- Decreased appetite
- Fatigue

Most side effects are mild to moderate and tend to improve as your body adjusts. Always report persistent or severe symptoms to your provider promptly.
